Business days vs calendar days: contract deadlines, Day 1 traps and leap years

Missing a cancellation window or response deadline rarely comes from bad arithmetic. It comes from three silent ambiguities: whether weekends count, whether the trigger date itself is Day 0 or Day 1, and what "one month" means at the end of January or February.

By Jaehyun Park (RAYV STUDIO), who builds and maintains Toolhub · Published 28 November 2026 · Updated 28 November 2026

30 calendar days vs 30 business days: 12 extra days on the calendar

Every five Monday-to-Friday business days consume one full seven-day week. A 30-business-day notice period is six full workweeks, so it spans at least 42 calendar days before any public holiday is even considered. Under Rule 6(a) of the U.S. Federal Rules of Civil Procedure, statutory periods stated in days default to calendar days — counting intermediate Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays — unless the rule or contract explicitly says "business days" or "working days".

Watch Saturday in U.S. mortgage disclosures. "Business day" does not even have a single federal definition. Under Regulation Z (12 CFR § 1026.2(a)(6)), the general definition is a day on which the creditor's offices are open to the public for substantially all business functions — typically Monday through Friday. Yet for the three-day right of rescission on a refinance and certain TILA-RESPA waiting periods, Regulation Z switches to its "specific" rule: all calendar days except Sundays and federal legal public holidays, meaning Saturday counts as a business day.

Rule 6(a)(1)(A) codifies the exclusive convention for federal court deadlines: "exclude the day of the event that triggers the period." By contrast, an employee booking Monday 2026-03-02 through Tuesday 2026-03-31 off from work is absent on both the start date and the end date — 22 weekdays out of the office, not 21. Whenever a merchant's return policy says "within 30 days of delivery", check whether the carrier's delivery date is treated as Day 0 (deadline 2026-04-01) or Day 1 (deadline 2026-03-31).

Month-end clamping and the 2028 leap-year boundary

Under the Gregorian calendar rule maintained by the U.S. Naval Observatory and NIST, years divisible by 4 are leap years (except century years not divisible by 400), making 2028 the next leap year with a 2028-02-29. That extra Tuesday changes both day-count intervals and month-end rollovers:

  • February interval length: 2027-02-01 to 2027-03-01 is 28 days (672 hours: 20 weekdays and 8 weekend days), whereas 2028-02-01 to 2028-03-01 is 29 days (696 hours: 21 weekdays and 8 weekend days).
  • End-of-month clamping: Adding 1 calendar month to 2027-01-31 clamps to 2027-02-28 because February 2027 has no 29th, 30th or 31st. Adding 1 calendar month to 2028-01-31 clamps to 2028-02-29.
  • Annual renewal from Leap Day: Adding 1 year to a contract signed on 2028-02-29 clamps to 2029-02-28, spanning 365 calendar days. Notice also that month addition is not associative: 2027-01-31 + 1 month gives 2027-02-28, and adding + 1 month again from 2027-02-28 lands on 2027-03-28, whereas adding 2 months directly to 2027-01-31 lands on 2027-03-31. Our date calculator always anchors multi-month jumps to the original start date so month-end dates do not drift inward.

Checklist before locking a contract or filing date

  • Write the calendar date next to the day count. "30 calendar days (on or before 1 April 2026)" removes all Day 0 / Day 1 disputes before anyone signs.
  • Check what happens when the final day is a weekend or holiday. Courts (FRCP Rule 6(a)(1)(C)) roll forward to the next court day, while many private option-exercise and lease-termination clauses require notice prior to expiration — rolling backward in practice if offices are closed.
  • Subtract public holidays manually after weekday counting. Pure weekday calculators skip Saturdays and Sundays; federal, state or bank holidays push a business-day deadline out by 1 additional weekday per holiday inside the window.
