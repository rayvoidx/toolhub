How the Chinese zodiac cycle works

The Chinese zodiac (生肖) assigns one of 12 animals to each year in a fixed, repeating order: Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, and Pig. Every 12th year brings the same animal back around — so if you know someone's animal, you already know every year they could have been born within a realistic lifetime. Layered on top of the 12 animals is a second, 10-year cycle of 5 elements (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water), each element lasting 2 years before moving on. Because 12 and 10 share a smallest common multiple of 60, the exact animal-and-element combination — say, a "Wood Rat" or a "Water Tiger" — only repeats once every 60 years, which is why a 60th birthday is traditionally a significant milestone in Chinese culture: it marks a full return to your birth combination.

Why the year doesn't start on January 1

Unlike the Gregorian calendar, the Chinese zodiac year begins on Chinese (Lunar) New Year, a date that shifts every year — usually somewhere between January 21 and February 20 — because it follows the lunisolar calendar rather than a fixed date. Anyone born between January 1 and that year's Chinese New Year is still considered part of the previous zodiac year. This calculator handles that automatically once you add your birth month and day, checking your date against a lookup table of actual Chinese New Year dates for 1930 through 2030. Without a month and day, the tool falls back to treating the calendar year as the zodiac year, which is correct for the vast majority of birthdays outside that January–February window.

Example 1 — a clean case. Someone born on July 4, 1990 doesn't need the Lunar New Year adjustment at all — July is nowhere near the boundary, so 1990 is simply the Year of the Metal Horse.

Example 2 — the boundary case. Chinese New Year 2023 fell on January 22. Someone born January 10, 2023 was born before that date, so despite the calendar saying "2023," their zodiac year is 2022 — the Year of the Water Tiger, not the Year of the Water Rabbit. Someone born January 25, 2023, just three days later, already belongs to 2023 and is a Rabbit.

Example 3 — the element cycle. 1984, 1996, 2008 and 2020 are all Rat years, but they are a Wood Rat, a Fire Rat, an Earth Rat, and a Metal Rat respectively — the element advances by two positions every 12 years until, after five trips around the animal cycle (60 years), it lands back on Wood again in 2044.

What "compatibility" means here

Traditional Chinese astrology groups the 12 animals into four "trine" sets of three animals said to share an easy rapport — Rat/Dragon/Monkey, Ox/Snake/Rooster, Tiger/Horse/Dog, and Rabbit/Goat/Pig — plus six "clash" pairs sitting exactly opposite each other in the cycle (Rat–Horse, Ox–Goat, Tiger–Monkey, Rabbit–Rooster, Dragon–Dog, Snake–Pig), traditionally described as needing more effort to get along. This calculator surfaces both for your result purely as a lighthearted cultural reference — it has no scientific basis and shouldn't guide real decisions about relationships, hiring, or compatibility.

Common questions this often raises

  • "I thought I was a different animal." This is almost always the Lunar New Year boundary — double-check your birth month and day, especially if you were born in January or the first three weeks of February.
  • Chinese, Korean and Japanese zodiacs mostly agree. The 12-animal cycle is shared across East Asian lunisolar calendars, though Vietnam swaps the Rabbit for a Cat and Japan traditionally uses the Boar instead of the domestic Pig — this calculator uses the standard Chinese animal set.
  • Element names vary by tradition. Some sources call the cycle "Wu Xing" and translate "Earth" as "Soil," but the five elements and their order are the same everywhere.

What is my Chinese zodiac animal, and how is it calculated from my birth year?

The Chinese zodiac cycles through 12 animals in a fixed order — Rat, Ox, Tiger, Rabbit, Dragon, Snake, Horse, Goat, Monkey, Rooster, Dog, Pig — repeating every 12 years. This calculator finds your position in that cycle from your birth year: for example, 1990 is the Year of the Horse, 2000 is the Year of the Dragon, and 2023 is the Year of the Rabbit. Why does Chinese New Year change my zodiac animal if I was born in January or February?

The Chinese zodiac year does not start on January 1 — it starts on Chinese (Lunar) New Year, which falls on a different date each year, usually between January 21 and February 20. If you were born before that date, you belong to the previous zodiac year. For example, Chinese New Year 2023 fell on January 22, so someone born on January 20, 2023 is actually a Tiger (2022's animal), not a Rabbit (2023's animal). What is a Chinese zodiac element, like Metal Ox or Water Tiger, and how is it determined?

Alongside the 12 animals, Chinese astrology cycles through 5 elements — Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water — each held for 2 consecutive years before moving to the next, so a full animal-and-element combination only repeats every 60 years. For example, 1984 was a Wood Rat, 1996 was a Fire Rat, 2008 was an Earth Rat, and 2020 was a Metal Rat — four different Rat years, four different elements. Are the Chinese zodiac compatibility matches (trines and clashes) based on science?

No — the "best match" and "needs extra effort" pairings shown here come from traditional Chinese astrology folklore, grouping animals into four-animal "trine" friendship sets and paired "clash" opposites six years apart in the cycle. They are a fun cultural tradition, not a scientific or psychological finding, and shouldn't be used to make real relationship decisions.
