How the Army body fat calculator works after the 2023 rewrite

Until June 2023 the Army tape test meant three measurements for men (neck and abdomen, against height) and four for women (neck, natural waist and hips). AR 600-9 replaced all of that with a single circumference: the abdomen, measured horizontally at the level of the navel, scored against height. One tape pull, one number, far less room for two graders to disagree. The Army made a second change at the same time: soldiers who score 540 or better on the ACFT with at least 80 points in every event skip the tape test entirely, which turns the whole Army Body Composition Program into a screen rather than a gate for fit soldiers.

This tool uses the one-site linear form: for men, body fat rises about 1.99 percentage points per inch of abdominal circumference and falls about 0.12 points per inch of height; for women the slopes are 1.51 and 0.156. The female equation uses the published constant. The male constant here is anchored to the published worksheet pass thresholds — a 70-inch soldier reaches the 22 percent line at roughly a 36-inch abdomen, matching the cut point the three-site method produced for the same body. Widely copied versions of the male equation carry a constant of -26.97, which returns about 32 percent for a 5 ft 10 in soldier with a 34-inch waist. That is roughly 15 points above what the same soldier scored under the old method and would fail almost everyone, so it is not used here. Treat every number below as an estimate, not a record.

Worked example 1: male, 5 ft 10 in, 34-inch abdomen, age 24

Height is 70 inches, abdomen 34 inches. Body fat = (1.99 x 34) - (0.12 x 70) - 41.5 = 67.66 - 8.4 - 41.5 = 17.8 percent. The standard for a man aged 21 to 27 is 22 percent, so the result is within standard with 4.2 points of margin. Practically, that soldier could add about two inches to the abdomen before hitting the limit, because each inch is worth roughly two points of body fat for men.

Worked example 2: female, 5 ft 4 in, 31-inch abdomen, age 30

Height 64 inches, abdomen 31 inches. Body fat = (1.51 x 31) - (0.156 x 64) - 9.15 = 46.81 - 9.98 - 9.15 = 27.7 percent, against a 34 percent standard for ages 28 to 39. That is 6.3 points of margin. Women carry a higher essential fat requirement, which is why the standards sit 10 points above the male ones in every band rather than being a softer version of the same line.

Worked example 3: over the line

Same 70-inch man with a 40-inch abdomen: (1.99 x 40) - 8.4 - 41.5 = 29.7 percent, which is 7.7 points over the 22 percent standard. Being over does not end a career. It triggers the supplemental body composition assessment path — DEXA, hydrostatic weighing, air displacement plethysmography or the approved alternative your installation offers — and if that assessment clears you, the tape number is set aside. Soldiers with heavy trunk musculature are the classic case: circumference equations cannot distinguish oblique and abdominal muscle from fat, so a strong lifter can tape high and DEXA clean.

Measurement protocol that actually changes your result

Half the arguments about the tape test are really arguments about technique. The protocol is specific: stand relaxed with arms at the sides, tape horizontal to the floor at the level of the navel, snug against the skin without compressing it. The reading is taken at the end of a normal relaxed exhale — not a forced exhale, not a held breath, not a sucked-in stomach. Three measurements are recorded to the nearest quarter inch or half centimetre and averaged, and if any one differs from the others by more than an inch, the set is repeated. A tape that rides half an inch high across the narrow part of the waist rather than sitting at the navel can shave a full inch off the reading, which is two points of body fat for a man. Measure the way it will be measured on the day.

What this number is not

It is not an official determination. Only a trained grader with a calibrated tape, recording on the DA Form 5501 worksheet, produces a number that goes in a record. It is also not a health measurement: circumference formulas are population fits, and they carry an error band of roughly three to five percentage points against DEXA for any individual. Use it the way the Army uses it — as a screen that tells you whether you are comfortably inside the standard, close enough to care about a half inch of tape placement, or far enough over that you should plan for the supplemental assessment.

Frequently asked questions

What changed in the 2023 Army tape test?

Since June 2023 AR 600-9 uses a single measurement: abdominal circumference at the navel, combined with height. The old three-site method for men (neck and waist) and four-site method for women (neck, waist and hips) are gone. Soldiers who max the ACFT with a score of 540 or better and at least 80 points per event are also exempt from the tape test entirely.

How accurate is the tape test compared with DEXA?

It is a screening tool, not a body composition measurement. Circumference equations typically land within about 3 to 5 percentage points of a DEXA scan, and they read high on soldiers with thick trunks or heavy lifting builds. That is why exceeding the tape standard triggers a supplemental assessment — DEXA, hydrostatic weighing or the Bod Pod — rather than an immediate flag.

How is the abdominal circumference measured correctly?

Stand relaxed with arms at your sides, tape horizontal to the floor at the level of the navel, snug but not compressing the skin. The reading is taken at the end of a normal relaxed exhale — not sucked in, not held. Three measurements are taken to the nearest quarter inch or half centimetre and averaged, which is what you should enter here.

What are the current Army body fat standards?

Maximum allowable body fat for men is 20% at ages 17 to 20, 22% at 21 to 27, 24% at 28 to 39 and 26% at 40 and over. For women it is 30%, 32%, 34% and 36% across the same age bands. The age used is your age on the date of the assessment.
