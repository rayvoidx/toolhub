How this moon phase calculator works

The Moon takes about 29.53 days to cycle from one new moon to the next — astronomers call this a synodic month. This calculator starts from a well-known reference new moon (January 6, 2000, 18:14 UTC) and measures how many days have elapsed since that reference point, wrapped to the current cycle. That elapsed time, the Moon's "age," is all it takes to estimate the phase, illumination, and the next full and new moon dates — no external astronomy service or API call required, so it works fully offline once the page has loaded.

  • Moon age = days since the last new moon, wrapped to a 29.53-day cycle.
  • Phase = which of 8 equal slices (~3.69 days each) the age falls into.
  • Illumination ≈ (1 − cos(2π × age ÷ 29.53)) ÷ 2, a smooth curve that is 0% at new moon, 100% at full moon.
  • Next full/new moon = the next date, at or after the one you picked, that lines up with the half-cycle (full) or full-cycle (new) point from the reference new moon.

Example — a full moon. Entering July 30, 2026 gives a moon age of about 15.7 days, landing in the Full Moon slice at roughly 99% illumination. The next new moon lands around August 13, 2026, and the next full moon after that around August 28, 2026.

Example — a new moon. Entering July 14, 2026 gives a moon age near the very end of the cycle (about 29.2 days), which the calculator reports as New Moon at roughly 0% illumination — the reference algorithm places the exact new moon later that same day.

Moon phases and lunar calendars around the world

Many traditional calendars are anchored to the Moon rather than the Sun alone. Korea's 음력 (eumnyeok) and Japan's 旧暦 (kyūreki), like the Chinese lunar calendar, are lunisolar systems: each month starts on or near a new moon, and the 15th of the lunar month lands close to a full moon. That's why harvest-moon festivals — Chuseok in Korea, Tsukimi (Otsukimi) in Japan, the Mid-Autumn Festival in China — always fall on or near a full moon, and why Lunar New Year always falls on a new moon. The Islamic Hijri calendar is fully lunar and traditionally starts each month at the sighting of a new crescent. This calculator doesn't convert a date into a lunisolar calendar date, but it's a fast way to check the underlying astronomical phase behind any of these traditions.

Accuracy and limitations

This tool uses the average synodic month, but the Moon's real orbit is elliptical, so any individual cycle can run several hours shorter or longer than the 29.53-day average — which is why results are accurate to roughly ±1 day, not to the minute. That's honest and good enough for personal planning, content calendars, or curiosity about tonight's sky, but it is not a substitute for a full astronomical ephemeris where you need celestial navigation, precise tide prediction, or a religious observance that requires an official sighting or authoritative announcement. For those, consult a national observatory, almanac, or the relevant religious authority.

Frequently asked questions

What moon phase is it today?

This calculator loads with today's date already selected and instantly shows the current moon phase, illumination percentage, and how many days old the Moon is in its current cycle. It also lists the next full moon and next new moon dates so you can plan ahead. Just open the page — no input required for today's answer.

How accurate is this moon phase calculator?

The Moon's phase is calculated from a known reference new moon and the average length of a synodic month (about 29.53 days), the same lunar-cycle math used by many simple moon calendars. This gives results accurate to roughly plus or minus one day for any date, past or future. It is not precise enough for celestial navigation, tide prediction, or religious observances that require exact timing — those need a full astronomical ephemeris.

What are the 8 moon phases and what do they mean?

A full lunar cycle moves through 8 named phases: New Moon, Waxing Crescent, First Quarter, Waxing Gibbous, Full Moon, Waning Gibbous, Last Quarter, and Waning Crescent. Waxing means the lit portion is growing night by night, and waning means it's shrinking. New Moon marks the start of the cycle, when the Moon is essentially invisible, and Full Moon marks the midpoint, when the entire face is lit. The reference strip below the calculator shows the glyph and a short description for each phase.

How does moon phase relate to lunar calendars like Korea's eumnyeok or Japan's kyūreki?

Traditional East Asian calendars such as Korea's 음력 (eumnyeok) and Japan's 旧暦 (kyūreki) are lunisolar: each calendar month begins on or near a new moon, and the 15th of the month falls close to a full moon. That's why holidays like Chuseok and Tsukimi (harvest moon festivals) and Lunar New Year always land on a new or full moon. This tool calculates the astronomical moon phase for any date — it doesn't convert dates to a lunisolar calendar, but it's a quick way to see why a given lunar-calendar date lines up with a particular moon phase.

Is the date I select sent to a server?

No. This moon phase calculator runs entirely in your browser — the date you pick and the calculated results never leave your device and nothing is uploaded or stored on a server. The only thing saved locally is the last date you looked up, kept in your browser's local storage so it's there if you come back.
