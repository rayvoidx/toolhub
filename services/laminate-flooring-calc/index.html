How much laminate flooring do I need?

Three numbers decide everything: the floor area, the waste allowance for cuts, and the square footage one box covers. Area times waste gives the material you actually have to own; dividing by the box coverage and rounding up gives the number of boxes the store will ring up. Every other line on your receipt — underlayment, transition strips, quarter round — follows from the same measurements.

Measure at the widest points of the room, wall to wall, and ignore the baseboard. Do not subtract kitchen islands, closets under 10 sq ft, or the footprint of a fridge: planks still run underneath or behind them, and the offcuts you would have saved are the ones you would have wasted anyway.

Worked example: a 16 ft by 13 ft living room

The floor area is 16 x 13 = 208 sq ft. It is a plain rectangle with a straight lay, so 10% waste is the sensible default: 208 x 1.10 = 228.8 sq ft of material. A typical laminate box covers 20 sq ft, so 228.8 / 20 = 11.44 boxes, which rounds up to 12 boxes. At $45 a box that is $540 in flooring.

The rest of the list: underlayment comes in 100 sq ft rolls, so 208 / 100 rounds up to 3 rolls — unless the planks already have foam bonded to the back. Two doorways means two transition strips (T-moulding between rooms of equal height, a reducer where laminate meets tile or vinyl). Twelve boxes cover 240 sq ft, so after roughly 20 sq ft disappears into cuts you finish with about one spare box in the garage. That is the outcome you want.

Choosing the waste percentage

LayoutWasteWhy
Square room, straight lay, no obstacles5%Offcuts from one row start the next
Standard rectangular room, a doorway or two10%The safe default for most jobs
Diagonal or herringbone, many alcoves15%Every row ends in an angled cut that cannot be reused
Long planks (over 48 in) in a small room15%Short leftover pieces fail the stagger rule

Two extra reasons to lean high rather than low: planks with a strong pattern get rejected on the floor for repeating too close together, and boards damaged in transit are common enough that most installers expect one or two per job. Waste is not sloppiness, it is the price of a floor that looks right.

Why the leftover box is cheap insurance

Laminate and vinyl are manufactured in dye lots. Two boxes of the same SKU made months apart can differ enough in tone that a single replacement plank stands out under window light. Stores also discontinue ranges constantly — the product you buy today may simply not exist when a dropped pan gouges a board in year three.

A spare box costs roughly $40 and stores flat under a bed. The alternative, once your range is gone, is re-laying the whole room. This is why the calculator rounds up to whole boxes and never suggests buying a partial one: the rounding is the insurance policy.

Underlayment or attached pad?

Underlayment does three jobs: it cushions the click-lock joints, it deadens footfall noise, and on concrete it blocks moisture. Standard foam rolls cover 100 sq ft; combination rolls with a vapour barrier are usually the same size and cost a few dollars more.

Many mid and upper range products ship with pad already bonded to the plank. Tick the attached-pad box and the calculator drops the rolls, because a second layer under a pre-padded plank makes the floor spongy and voids most warranties. One important exception: over a concrete slab, below grade or on the ground floor, you still need a 6 mil polyethylene vapour barrier taped at the seams even with attached pad. Check the installation sheet — it will say so explicitly.

Limits of this estimate

The calculator assumes one rectangle. For an L-shaped room, split it into two rectangles, run each one, and add the boxes; do not add the areas first, because rounding each section up is what protects you. Coverage per box varies by product from about 12 sq ft for wide-plank premium ranges to 30 sq ft for economy vinyl, so always read the actual label rather than trusting a default. Transition strips are sold in 36 in lengths, which covers a standard 32 in doorway with a trim; wider openings need two. Finally, none of this covers stairs, which are priced by tread and riser, or the subfloor levelling compound you may discover you need once the old carpet comes up.

Frequently asked questions

Why does the calculator round up to whole boxes?

Flooring is sold by the box and stores rarely split one. Dye lots also shift between production runs, so a box bought six months later can be a visibly different shade. Rounding up and keeping the leftovers means a damaged plank can be swapped from the same lot instead of relaying the floor.

Which direction should the planks run?

The usual rule is to lay planks parallel to the longest wall, or in line with the light from the main window, which hides the seams. In narrow hallways always run them along the length. Leave a 1/4 inch (6 mm) expansion gap at every wall and fixed object, hidden afterwards by baseboard or quarter round.

Does this work for vinyl plank flooring too?

Yes. Laminate and click-lock LVP or SPC are both sold in boxes with the coverage printed on the label, so the math is identical — enter the sq ft per box of whichever product you are pricing. Laminate boxes usually cover 18 to 24 sq ft; vinyl plank boxes run about 20 to 30.

Do I need to acclimate the flooring before installing?

Most manufacturers ask for 48 hours with the unopened boxes lying flat in the room where they will be installed, at normal living temperature and humidity. Skipping it is the most common cause of gaps and buckling months later. Check the warranty sheet — a few rigid-core vinyl products need no acclimation at all.
