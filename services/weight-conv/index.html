Mass units across three traditions: metric, imperial, and Korean

Weighing something sounds simple until the units come from different worlds: a bathroom scale in kilograms or pounds, a UK body weight in stone, a recipe in ounces, freight in tonnes, and — at a Korean market or jeweler — food in 근 (geun) and gold in 돈 (don). This converter defines every unit in terms of the gram and converts through that base, so one entry appears in all nine units at once, including the two Korean units most global tools leave out.

How to use it

  1. Enter the value.
  2. Choose the From and To units; ⇄ swaps them, and the table below shows every unit simultaneously.
  3. Your last unit pair is saved locally so the tool opens ready for the same conversion.

The exact factors (no rounded 2.2)

The international avoirdupois pound is defined as exactly 0.45359237 kg, so 1 kg = 2.2046226218 lb — not the schoolbook 2.2, which drifts noticeably over large weights. From the pound: 1 lb = 16 ounces (1 oz = 28.349523125 g) and 1 stone = 14 lb = 6.35029318 kg. The Korean units are metric by decree: 1 근 (geun) for food is 600 g, and 1 돈 (don) is 3.75 g, the standard for weighing gold and other precious metals.

Worked examples

1. Body weight, pounds to kilograms. 150 lb × 0.45359237 = 68.04 kg. Going the other way, 70 kg × 2.2046226218 = 154.32 lb. The exact factor matters: using a flat 2.2 would give 154.0 lb, a 0.3 lb error that only grows with weight.
2. A recipe in ounces. 8 oz of flour × 28.349523125 = 226.8 g, and 1.5 lb of sugar = 1.5 × 453.59237 = 680.4 g. Type the ounces once to read grams for a metric kitchen scale.
3. Gold priced by the don. A "one-don" ring is 3.75 g, so five don of gold = 5 × 3.75 = 18.75 g ≈ 0.603 troy ounces. Meanwhile 2 근 of beef at the market = 2 × 600 = 1,200 g = 1.2 kg.

Common mistakes

  • Rounding the pound to 2.2. Fine for a rough guess, wrong for anything precise — use the exact 0.45359237 kg factor this tool applies.
  • Confusing the avoirdupois ounce with the troy ounce. Precious metals are traded in troy ounces (31.1035 g), not the 28.35 g avoirdupois ounce here. For gold, the don is the relevant traditional unit; convert to grams first, then to troy ounces if you need them.
  • Assuming one "geun." The food geun is 600 g, but a herbal-medicine geun is traditionally 375 g. This tool uses the everyday food value; specialist contexts may differ.
  • Mass vs weight. Kilograms and pounds here measure mass. Your "weight" as a force changes with gravity; on the Moon your mass in kg is unchanged even though you feel lighter.

Precision and scope

Every result keeps up to 10 significant figures and trims trailing zeros, switching to scientific notation for very large or very small magnitudes so nothing is lost to rounding. The converter handles mass only — it does not price gold or convert to troy ounces, and it cannot turn a volume like a cup of flour into grams, which depends on density.

Frequently asked questions

How many pounds are in a kilogram?

1 kilogram = 2.2046226218 pounds, and 1 pound = 0.45359237 kg exactly — the exact value of the international avoirdupois pound, not a rounded 2.2. So going kg to lbs, 70 kg ≈ 154.32 lb; going pounds to kg, 150 lb ≈ 68.04 kg. This weight converter uses the exact factor, so kg-to-lbs and lbs-to-kg both stay accurate.

What about ounces and stone?

1 ounce = 28.349523125 grams (so converting ounces to grams is handy for recipes and postage), 1 pound = 16 ounces, and 1 stone = 14 pounds = 6.35029318 kg. Stone is the everyday body-weight unit in the UK and Ireland, so 11 st = 154 lb ≈ 69.85 kg. Type a value once to see stone to kg and every other unit at the same time.

What are 근 (geun) and 돈 (don)?

They are traditional Korean mass units. One 근 (geun) is 600 g, used to weigh food such as meat and vegetables at markets. One 돈 (don) is 3.75 g, the standard unit for weighing gold and other precious metals at jewelers. Most global converters leave them out, so this tool adds both next to the metric and imperial units.

How precise are the results?

Every conversion keeps up to 10 significant figures, then trims trailing zeros for readability. Very large or very small numbers (roughly 1e15 and above, or below 1e-9) switch to scientific notation, so nothing is silently rounded away to zero. All values use the exact SI definition of each unit.

Is my input sent to a server?

No. This weight converter runs entirely in your browser — the number you type is never uploaded anywhere. The only thing saved is your last chosen unit pair (for example pounds → kilograms), kept in your browser's local storage so it is ready next time. The value itself is not stored.
